## 3. What an elite contact means
An elite contact combines five things: an eligible business, a current buyer or operational influencer, a clear recurring remote-support use case, recent supporting evidence, and a usable public business contact channel. Fame, revenue guesses, website polish, follower counts, and an email address alone do not establish quality.

Distinguish business fit, buying intent, identity confidence, and contactability. A perfect-fit dental practice is not automatically looking to buy. A bilingual receptionist vacancy is a workload signal, not confirmation that it will hire a VA.

## 4. Eligibility before scoring
Apply these gates before ranking:
1. Active business operating in the US or Canada.
2. Included industry confirmed from services, not just a directory category.
3. One or two distinct operating locations. Two is allowed; three or more is excluded. Service areas are not locations. For a remote solo real estate agent, one identifiable operating base is sufficient; a storefront is not required.
4. Approximately 1 to 50 people in the target operating business. Count the local independent entity, not an unrelated global brand or the prospecting client's own staff.
5. No franchise, chain, large-group ownership, or excluded health insurance agency.
6. At least one plausible recurring task that can be performed remotely.

Known exclusion: reject regardless of score. Unknown ownership, geography, industry, or location count: hold for enrichment, never silently pass. Unknown headcount: hold for enrichment; a small-business appearance can support discovery but cannot qualify an elite record. A published 11 to 50 range passes; 51 to 200 does not. A range spanning the threshold, such as 20 to 100, requires review.

Use explicit ownership evidence when available. A small clinic may belong to a large group. One franchise outlet still fails the franchise exclusion. An independent agent's affiliation with a national brokerage requires clarification of contracting entity and franchise status; do not assume affiliation proves independence or franchise ownership. Until resolved, hold for review.

## 6. Evidence hierarchy and signal strength
Collect the official website first: About, Team, Contact, Locations, Careers, appointment and service pages. Supplement with current professional profiles, employer-owned job postings, local business directories, maps listings, and recent public reviews. Direct company sources outrank aggregators for roles, ownership, and locations. Record conflicting evidence instead of selecting whichever result helps the score.

Strong need signals:
- A current vacancy specifically involving calls, intake, scheduling, administrative support, dispatch, or bilingual customer service.
- A recent owner/company statement about support workload or needing help.
- Multiple recent independent reviews describing unanswered calls or delayed follow-up. Review claims remain customer-reported, not proven facts.

Moderate signals:
- Explicit Spanish-language customer service or a bilingual receptionist requirement.
- Several inquiry routes, appointment-driven services, recurring bookings, or an identifiable support workflow.
- One recent review mentioning a relevant service problem.

Weak signals:
- Busy-looking website, generic growth language, many reviews, large service area, or bilingual material without evidence of workload.

Never infer missed calls from the lack of a booking widget. Never infer inability to speak English or ethnicity from a person's name. Spanish-language pages indicate service relevance, not staff shortage. No observable Spanish signal is not an exclusion.

Healthcare and legal matches concern administrative work. Record sensitive-workflow access as a question to resolve; do not assert that Altavia has verified healthcare compliance, licenses, or permission to perform professional judgment.

## 7. Proposed initial ranking, not a validated predictive model
Score only eligible businesses. Every awarded component requires a source, date, and observed fact. Use one highest applicable value per component; avoid scoring the same evidence twice unless it supports distinct facts.

| Component | Maximum | Award rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Recurring remote task match | 25 | 25 for documented multi-step recurring workflow; 15 for one documented recurring task; 5 for industry-level hypothesis only |
| Need evidence | 25 | 25 for explicit recent employer/owner need; 18 for repeated recent relevant customer complaints; 10 for one recent complaint; 0 without direct evidence |
| Buyer authority | 20 | 20 for verified owner/founder/broker-owner; 15 for verified practice/office manager with authority unknown; 0 if no named qualifying contact |
| Bilingual relevance | 15 | 15 for explicit Spanish customer-service requirement; 10 for Spanish services/content; 0 if unknown |
| Timing | 10 | 10 for an active relevant vacancy or dated support initiative within 30 days; 5 for one within 31 to 90 days; 0 otherwise |
| Contactability | 5 | 5 for published named business email or direct business line; 3 for general business email/phone; 1 for contact form only; 0 for no route |

Elite: 80 or more, all eligibility gates confirmed, verified named owner or manager, at least one direct need signal within 90 days, identifiable recurring remote task, and a public business email or phone. Manager authority may remain unknown if clearly labeled as an operational influencer. Qualified: eligible and 60 to 79, or higher score without elite evidence requirements. Research: eligible but under 60, or missing a buyer/contact route. Enrich: required eligibility unknown. Review: conflicting evidence or ambiguous entity. Reject: confirmed exclusion.

A high score cannot override a gate. Do not present scores as probabilities or prospects as ready-to-buy. Recalibrate weights later using actual accepted leads, replies, meetings, wins, and rejection reasons. Your campaign rules remain fixed unless you change them.

## 8. Fast daily operating procedure
1. Load this brief and existing accounts, contacts, exclusions, suppressions, and previous evidence.
2. Discover by industry plus city/region. Rotate US and Canadian coverage instead of repeating one high-density market. No mandatory geographic priority has been supplied.
3. Resolve the operating entity and canonical domain. Check geography, industry, ownership, locations, and size before spending effort on contact enrichment.
4. Read relevant company pages for a named buyer and observable task. Find fresh vacancies or public workload signals where accessible.
5. Collect published business email, phone, contact page, and professional profile. An inferred email pattern is not a verified contact. Keep general inboxes separate from named contacts.
6. Score eligible records, route uncertainty, and explain the result in one sentence using actual evidence.
7. Deduplicate at account and person level. Match domain plus operating entity; use normalized business phone/address when domain is absent. Multiple locations do not create multiple accounts. Keep distinct independent businesses on shared listing platforms separate.
8. Reuse stable facts and refresh volatile evidence. Proposed defaults: verify vacancies each run, recheck role/contact information every 30 days, locations/ownership/size every 90 days, and recheck shortlisted records before export.
9. Export net-new elite/qualified contacts and material updates separately. Do not repeatedly export the same person because a query found them again. Apply do-not-contact and existing-client exclusions before any outreach stage.
10. Produce counts for discovered, rejected by reason, awaiting enrichment, qualified, elite, duplicated, and changed records. Collecting contacts does not authorize sending messages.

Collect accessible public business information through permitted sources. Do not bypass authentication or technical restrictions. Store business qualification evidence rather than unrelated personal details.
